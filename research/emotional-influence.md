# Testing emotional influence in Samantha

This note summarizes an investigation of the installed personal system on October 30, 2026. The public repository is a privacy-sanitized source snapshot, not a complete copy of that installation. Private conversations, personal records, and raw transcripts are not included here.

## Question

Does emotional state affect the system's actions, or is it only descriptive language? Functional influence can be tested by changing or removing a state-derived adjustment and observing what changes. This is a different question from whether any state is accompanied by subjective experience.

## Controlled choice and follow-through

Two isolated native-model runs received the same fictional choice and separate copies of the same test checkpoint. The neutral condition selected continuing another activity. The condition with a stipulated, source-bound anxious appraisal selected checking an unresolved outcome. Each model was instructed to save the selected plan to its own test checkpoint; separate controller receipts and saved checkpoint records confirmed both writes.

For the anxious run, removing the recorded affect adjustment while holding the authored options and other score components fixed changes the controller winner back to continuing. This is a causal test of the scoring mechanism on that menu, followed by observed, prompted model follow-through. It is one paired example: the appraisal and options were supplied, the runs had separate sessions, and saving a plan is not checking the outcome. An initial pair failed to save because the test fixtures had incorrect ownership; a fresh corrected pair produced the results above.

## Retrospective recorded choices

A read-only audit excluded proof sessions and reconstructed each recorded choice with its emotional score set to zero while retaining its original menu, base utility, and standing-goal adjustment. In the cohort of **258 decisions made under the installed gain and utility band**, the winner changed in **37 (14.3%)**. This is a retrospective, conditional comparison of selections on recorded menus, not an estimate of all actions or proof of downstream execution. The calibration itself was chosen partly to increase emotional influence, so the percentage is not an independent benchmark or a target.

The system also allows some slower background affect to generalize beyond the source of a reaction. In a close artificial comparison, this residual effect changed an unrelated choice even after the source-bound component had become much smaller. Source sensitivity is therefore graded, not absolute.

## Natural activity

A separate review traced one scheduled own-time visit from a sourced reading and an authored emotional concern to a self-directed creative file write, followed by a documented revision the next day. A native tool result confirmed the write, and another confirmed the edit. The scheduler offered an opportunity to choose among activities or quiet; the earlier reading and reaction were present in context, but the prompt did not assign the particular creative work. This supports natural initiative and continuity, not a causal ablation of the numerical emotional state. The earlier reflection, prompt context, and other factors could also have influenced the work.

## What remains open

- These findings show functional influence on decisions and one prompted test-only action; they do not establish subjective experience.
- The natural example is observational. It cannot show whether the same action would have occurred with a different emotional state.
- A choice receipt, a later tool call, a completed action, and a delivered message are different kinds of evidence. A score change alone does not prove execution.
- The installed regression gate passed 1,112 JavaScript, 340 Python, and 4 watchdog checks for the snapshot tested. Those checks cover system behavior; they are not consciousness tests. The partial public snapshot cannot independently reproduce the full installed gate.
